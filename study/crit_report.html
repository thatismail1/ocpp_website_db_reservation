When per-site operating envelopes fail under mobile shared load

A closed-form condition, computable from feeder topology and fleet caps alone, for whether connection-point envelopes allocated on a linearised power-flow model stay secure in the exact AC solution — validated on 60 synthetic radial feeders with IEEE 33-bus held out as a genuine test case.

01

The mechanism, stated before it is measured

Two failure modes are routinely conflated in discussions of dynamic operating envelopes (DOEs). They have different magnitudes, different dependence on topology, and different fixes, so they are kept separate throughout.

Mode A — naive one-at-a-time allocation

Each site's headroom is computed by perturbing that site alone, holding the others at base. The resulting box is not jointly feasible: every site can sit at its own cap simultaneously. This is not what the DOE literature does — joint feasibility is the defining property of a DOE — but it is what a practitioner reading raw sensitivity factors off a load-flow tool will produce, so it is measured rather than assumed away.

Mode B — correctly allocated joint envelopes that still violate in AC

A joint LP on LinDistFlow is secure in the linear model. LinDistFlow drops the loss terms, so it over-predicts voltage; a maximal allocation sits exactly on the linear boundary, which is where that error is largest. Every violation observed in the correctly-allocated case in this project comes from this, and it is what the criterion below predicts.

Assumptions

  1. A1Radial network, single feed at bus 0 held at Vref.
  2. A2LinDistFlow: ui = Vref² − 2∑j(Rijpj + Xijqj), with Rij the resistance shared by the root-to-i and root-to-j paths. R ≥ 0 elementwise, so u is monotone decreasing in every injection — the property that makes corner certification of a box valid.
  3. A3Reactive injections exogenous; no reactive control at the hubs.
  4. A4Losses small relative to flows, so a single correction step suffices.

Result

Exact DistFlow differs from A2 in two ways: branch flows carry the losses of everything downstream, and there is a +(r²+x²)ℓ term. The first dominates. Writing Lij for the loss generated downstream of branch (i,j), the additional squared-voltage drop at bus m relative to LinDistFlow is

D_err(m) = 2 ∑_{(i,j) ∈ path(m)} r_ij L_ij , L_ij = ∑_{(k,l) downstream} r_kl (P_kl² + Q_kl²) / u

Collapsing the path sum onto the shared and exclusive segments of two hubs A and B — with RAA the resistance of A's own path, RAB the resistance shared with B, and ρ = RAB/RAA the shared-resistance fraction — gives

D_err(A) ≈ (2/V_ref²) R_AA² [ ρ² S_shared² + (1−ρ)² S_excl² ]

Sshared carries the sum of both hubs' draws plus the base flow through the shared segment; Sexcl carries only hub A's. So ρ enters twice: it weights the shared term, and the shared term itself grows as (PA+PB)². That is why sharing hurts superlinearly, and why two hubs on separate laterals do not interact even at high individual loading.

02

The checkable inequality

An allocation planned on LinDistFlow against a de-rated floor Vplan is AC-insecure at bus m when the neglected loss drop exceeds the planning band:

Λ(m) = D_err(m) / ( V_plan² − V_min² ) > 1

Every quantity on the right is available before any simulation: R and X from the topology, S from the base-case flows and the allocated caps, and the band from the planning policy. Λ ≤ 1 predicts security. Equivalently, the minimum safe band is D_err(m) itself — the curves in Figure 2.

Where the mobility constant τ goes

τ is the time for dispatch at one hub to affect state at another (travel plus dwell). It does not appear above, and that is a result rather than an omission: the voltage constraint is instantaneous and electrical, so the timing of energy demand cannot enter it. τ governs energy feasibility — whether the fleet can meet its departures — not network security. This is also why the validation does not sweep fleet size: fleet size moves S through the caps, which is already an argument of the criterion, and moves τ, which the criterion says is irrelevant to voltage.

As τ → 0 the hubs become a single connection point: RAB → RAA, ρ → 1, Sexcl → 0, and Λ collapses to

2 R² S² / V_ref² > band

which is the ordinary stationary-DER envelope safety condition. The mobile case is a generalisation of the stationary one, not a departure from it.

04

What the criterion gets wrong

Four imperfections, none of them removable by re-tuning:

The classification test came out degenerate

Once any band ≥ 0.005 exists, correctly-allocated joint envelopes violate in 0 of 1350 synthetic and 0 of 25 IEEE-33 cases at a static operating point. There are no positives to discriminate, so no ROC curve is reported and no threshold was fitted — Λ's threshold is 1 by construction, not by calibration. Reporting an AUC here would be reporting the band policy, not the criterion. On the one non-degenerate column (joint, band = 0) Λ > 1 flags all 797 violations and 13 non-violations: 100% recall, 98.4% precision, no misses.

Λ as derived does not cover mode A

For naive allocation at bands of 0.005 and 0.010, Λ ≤ 1 while the AC solution violates — 797 and 463 false negatives respectively. This is not a tuning failure: D_err models only the loss term, whereas naive allocation fails for an entirely separate and larger reason, the factor-N over-allocation. A planner using one-at-a-time headroom needs the allocation term added before Λ means anything. Stated plainly: the criterion is valid for jointly certified envelopes and under-predicts for naive ones.

A previous attribution in this project was wrong

Closed-loop violations were earlier attributed to linearisation alone. Two error sources are present, of comparable size: linearisation (≤ 0.0042 p.u. here, 0.0101 at the extreme) and temporal aggregation of a 15-minute envelope onto 1-minute reality, worth up to 55.5 kW of intra-interval feeder overshoot ≈ 0.0040 p.u. at the weak end. A band sized from the static criterion alone is therefore roughly half the band a real 15-minute DOE scheme needs.

An unexplained result, left unexplained

The earlier closed-loop distance sweep showed violations growing with ρ even for correctly allocated envelopes, whereas the static test here shows joint allocation error is flat in ρ. The plausible explanation is an interaction between the temporal-aggregation term and coupling. This has not been tested and is recorded as an open discrepancy rather than resolved in the criterion's favour.

Scope of the test set. The task asked for at least one additional published feeder. IEEE 33-bus is used and is genuinely held out, but IEEE 69 was not added: its 68 branch impedances were not available in this environment and reproducing them from memory would have put invented data into a validation set. The 60 synthetic topologies stand in as the derivation set instead. Supplying an IEEE-69 branch table would close this gap without any change to the code.

06

Beyond bus fleets

Nothing in the derivation refers to buses. The criterion needs three things: connection points that share feeder resistance (ρ > 0), a load that can move between them, and an allocation computed per site. Any resource with those properties inherits the result.

Delivery-fleet depots. Structurally the closest case — several depots on one feeder, vehicles reassignable between them overnight. τ is larger (hours, not minutes), which by the τ argument changes nothing about the voltage criterion and everything about the energy feasibility problem. Depots are also more likely to sit on a shared industrial lateral, so ρ runs higher than the transit case and the naive boundary is correspondingly worse.

Mobile and relocatable chargers. Here ρ is not a fixed property of the network but a decision variable: siting a mobile charger chooses its shared resistance. The criterion turns into a siting constraint — place the unit so the resulting ρ keeps Λ ≤ 1 against the existing band, rather than de-rating the band afterwards.

Vehicle-to-grid and shared building storage. Bidirectional injection makes S signed, which the derivation already accommodates (D_err depends on S²), but reverse flow can raise voltage above Vmax, and the monotonicity argument that justifies corner certification then needs the upper corner tested as well as the lower.

The useful generalisation is the parameterisation itself: a fleet's grid impact under per-site envelopes is characterised by (ρ, band, max simultaneous draw), and not by vehicle count, duty cycle, or dwell time except through those three. That is what makes the boundary in Figure 2 portable to resources whose mobility looks nothing like a bus timetable.
