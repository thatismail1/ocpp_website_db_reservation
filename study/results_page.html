When Envelopes Need to Know the Fleet

Dynamic operating envelopes are allocated per connection point, per interval. That is sufficient for a bus fleet whose charging hubs are electrically independent — and it fails, on both network security and transit service, once the hubs contend for the same feeder capacity. The crossover is measurable, and it falls at a shared-path impedance ratio between 0.28 and 0.52.

§ 1 — The experiment

One variable: how much network the two hubs share

Hub A sits at bus 18, the end of the longest lateral. Hub C is moved inward along that same lateral — buses 17, 16, 14, 12, 8, 4 — and finally onto a separate lateral at bus 22. Coupling is measured as the shared path resistance between the two hub buses divided by hub A's own path resistance to the substation, running from 0.934 (almost the entire path in common) down to 0.008 (nothing in common but the first branch).

Hub C is modelled as a bare en-route charging point: no building load, no canopy PV. Moving it therefore leaves the base case identical at every placement — minimum voltage 0.9572 p.u., zero violations, before any bus charges. Feeder loading is 0.60 and the fleet is 16 vehicles throughout. Electrical coupling is the only quantity that changes.

Three allocation methods are compared. Two are the standard dynamic operating envelope, connection-specific and per-interval, computed with no access to the timetable or vehicle state of charge, under the two objectives the literature uses: proportional fairness and maximum efficiency. The third is the fleet-aware allocation, which coordinates the four hubs against the fleet's actual energy requirements. All three are evaluated on nonlinear AC power flow.

§ 2 — Reading it

The rule cuts both ways, and that is the useful part

Above the crossover, no envelope setting works

At coupling 0.934 the proportional-fairness envelope leaves 161 under-voltage bus-minutes; the efficiency envelope leaves 162 and misses 45 departures. Widening the security band does not rescue it — tested separately, a wider band cuts violations from 91 to 15 but pushes missed departures from 0 to 10, and a wider band still trades further in the same direction. The two objectives cannot both be met, because a connection-specific envelope has no way to express give hub A the shared capacity now, a bus is berthed there and hub C is empty. The fleet-aware allocation holds zero violations at 18.7 kWh of shortfall.

Below the crossover, the simple method is not merely adequate — it is better

At coupling 0.008 every method is secure, and the connection-specific envelope delivers 8.38 MWh against the fleet-aware 7.65 MWh. The coordination layer is conservative, and where the network does not force hubs to compete, that conservatism is pure loss. A planner with hubs on separate laterals should not buy a coordination platform.

The crossover is where the design rule lives

Between coupling 0.276 and 0.515 both envelope methods cross from zero violations to substantial violation. Since the coupling ratio is computed directly from the feeder impedance data — shared path resistance over own path resistance — a network operator can evaluate it for a proposed hub layout before building anything.

§ 3 — Honest accounting

What this supersedes, and what is still missing

This supersedes two earlier readings of the same system. An earlier version claimed the framework's advantage lay in a jointly-certified envelope, an AC-corrected linearisation and a forecast margin. All three are occupied by prior work — the maximal admissible hyperrectangle is Liu & Braslavsky (IEEE Trans. Power Systems 2023), the forecast-margin result is Project EDGE (2023) with field data, and conservative linearisation is a developed literature. A later version then concluded, from a topology where the four hubs sat on separate laterals, that fleet-awareness bought nothing at all. That conclusion was an artifact of the test case; this sweep locates the regime it missed.

An earlier attempt at this sweep was wrong. It recalibrated feeder load at each placement to hold base voltage constant, which confounded coupling with total loading and produced a reversed trend. The bare-hub design removes the confound by leaving the base case untouched.

Still missing

  • One feeder, one synthetic timetable. The crossover value of 0.28–0.52 is measured on the IEEE 33-bus system with a constructed transit schedule. Whether the ratio itself generalises is untested.
  • No analytical condition. The crossover is observed, not derived. A condition on the sensitivity matrix predicting it would turn this from a case study into a method.
  • Two fairness objectives out of a family. α-fairness and max-min are not tested, and the operating-envelope community will expect them.
  • Fleet-aware reference at four placements of seven, on cost grounds.
  • Forecast uncertainty is not exercised in this sweep; all runs use perfect forecasts, so the comparison isolates allocation structure rather than robustness.
