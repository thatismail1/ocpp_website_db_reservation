When per-site operating envelopes fail under mobile shared flexible loads

Two additive error terms — the loss term LinDistFlow omits, and the intra-interval drift a 15-minute envelope cannot see — combine into one inequality computable from topology and fleet parameters alone. The second term is what makes envelope failure a coupling phenomenon rather than a refresh-rate one, and it closes the discrepancy that was open in the previous version of this work.

01

The result in one paragraph

A per-site envelope allocated on a linearised model and refreshed every 15 minutes carries two errors. Derr, the omitted loss term, is quadratic in power, weighted by ρ², and — measured at a static operating point — flat in ρ. Dagg, the intra-interval drift term, is linear in the drift, weighted by ρ¹, and zero at a static point. The earlier contradiction (closed-loop violations grew with ρ; the static criterion said they should not) was Dagg missing from the index. Adding it lifts per-minute recall against the AC solver from 5.6% to 82.2% at a 0.005 band and from 0% to 79.6% at 0.010. Across 42 closed-loop runs on two feeders — 60 480 simulated minutes, 28 of them on configurations no threshold was fitted on — Λtotal produced zero false positives. The misses are all shallower than the first-order model's own residual.

The reading this settles: not “15-minute refresh is too slow, independently of mobility”. At ρ ≤ 0.276 the same refresh cadence, the same fleet and the same drift magnitude produce zero violations; violations appear only once the drift propagates through shared resistance to a bus already near the floor. The cadence is necessary but not sufficient — coupling is what converts it into a violation.
02

Derivation

Assumptions

  1. A1Radial network, single feed at bus 0 held at Vref.
  2. A2LinDistFlow: ui = Vref² − 2∑j(Rijpj + Xijqj), with Rij the resistance shared by the root-to-i and root-to-j paths.
  3. A3Reactive injections exogenous; no reactive control at the hubs.
  4. A4Losses small relative to flows, so a single correction step suffices.

T1 — corner certification holds at N hubs

Rij is a sum of positive branch resistances, so R ≥ 0 elementwise and ui(P) = ui(0) − 2∑hRihPh is non-increasing in every coordinate of P. A function non-increasing in each coordinate attains its minimum over a box ∏h[0, P̄h] at the maximal corner. One evaluation at the all-caps corner therefore certifies the whole box, for any N. Nothing in the argument is pairwise.

T2 — exact N-hub form of Derr

Start from Derr(m) = 2∑k∈path(m) rkLk with Lk = (1/u)∑l≽k rlSl². Exchange the order of summation: branch l contributes to every k on path(m) upstream of it, and the total resistance of those k is exactly Rm,l. Hence, with no pairwise collapse and no approximation,

D_err(m) = (2/u) ∑_l r_l R_ml S_l² (exact)

Substituting Sl = Bl + ∑h1[l∈path(h)]Ph and expanding gives a quadratic form in the allocated caps:

D_err(m) = (1/u) [ P′ C⁽ᵐ⁾ P + 2 b⁽ᵐ⁾′ P + c⁽ᵐ⁾ ] C⁽ᵐ⁾_hh′ = ∑_{l ∈ path(h) ∩ path(h′)} r_l R_ml

C(m) is a Gram matrix of the vectors (√(rlRml) 1[l∈path(h)])l, so it is positive semidefinite: Derr is convex in the caps. The two-hub expression — D_err(A) ≈ (2/Vref²)RAA²[ρ²Sshared² + (1−ρ)²Sexcl²] — is the H = 2 case, and the ρ² weighting is C(m)AB/C(m)AA in the limit of short exclusive segments. The implementation was checked against the independent loss-propagation routine at H = 2, 3, 4, 5 on IEEE-33, IEEE-69 and synthetic feeders: maximum relative difference 5×10−16, and min eig(C) > 0 in every case.

T3 — the temporal-aggregation term

An envelope allocated once per interval of length Δ is planned against interval-mean injections p̄. Within the interval the plant realises p(t). Under A2 the resulting extra drop is exact and first order:

D_agg(m,t) = (2/S_b) ∑_j [ R_mj δp_j(t) + X_mj δq_j(t) ], δp_j(t) = p_j(t) − p̄_j

If injection j drifts at bounded rate |dpj/dt| ≤ γj, then maxt|δpj| ≤ γjΔ/2, giving the planning-time bound computable before any simulation:

D_agg(m) ≤ (Δ / 2S_b) ∑_j [ R_mj γ_j + X_mj γᶵ_j ]

Collapsed onto two hubs with A at m: D_agg(A) ≤ (Δ/2Sb)RAA[ργshared + (1−ρ)γexcl]. Note the structural contrast, which is the whole point: Derr is quadratic in power and carries ρ²; Dagg is linear in drift and carries ρ¹. They are additive at first order.

The inequality

Λ_total(m,t) = ( D_err(m,t) + D_agg(m,t) ) / ( u_lin(m, p̄(t)) − V_min² ) > 1

The denominator is the certified slack: what the allocation's own linear model promised at bus m, which is ≥ Vplan² − Vmin² and equals it when the allocation binds. At a static operating point Dagg = 0 and the whole thing reduces to the earlier Λ. No parameter in either term is fitted — Dagg is the exact first-order response to a measured deviation and Derr is exact by T2 — so the holdout question does not arise for the index itself, and the held-out feeders below test the derivation rather than a calibration.

Where τ goes

τ, the time for dispatch at one hub to affect state at another, still does not enter the voltage criterion: the constraint is instantaneous and electrical. What the closed-loop result adds is that τ is not irrelevant to drift: it is the fleet's arrival and departure pattern that makes the hub-bus deviation δp large in the first place. τ sets γ, and γ enters through Dagg. As τ → 0 the hubs merge, ρ → 1, and Λtotal collapses to the stationary single-site condition 2R²S²/Vref² + (ΔR/Sb)γ > band.

Closed loop — three sets, one protocol

Every closed-loop run is a full day: proportionally-fair DOE allocation refreshed every 15 minutes, 1-minute MPC re-solved every 5 minutes, hub C a bare en-route point moved along the lateral so the base case is identical at every placement and coupling is the only variable. Λ and Λtotal are evaluated at every minute at the bus the AC solution says binds; every violation is read off the backward/forward sweep. Three sets, 42 runs, 60 480 simulated minutes:

  1. FITIEEE-33, 7 placements × 2 bands. This is the set the 0.85 operating threshold was chosen on.
  2. HOLDIEEE-33, 5 new placements (ρ = 0.194, 0.369, 0.463, 0.647, 0.750) plus 2 placements at a larger fleet (20 vehicles rather than 16), × 2 bands. None used to pick the threshold.
  3. IEEE-69Second published feeder, 7 placements × 2 bands. FEEDER_LOAD 0.75 so the base-case minimum voltage matches IEEE-33's (0.9567 vs 0.9572) — the operating point is matched, the criterion is not tuned.

The threshold generalises on precision, not on recall. Precision and false-positive rate are preserved perfectly — zero false positives in all 60 480 minutes across all three sets. Recall degrades from 0.955/0.941 on the fitting set to 0.852/0.856 on the IEEE-33 holdout, and rises to 1.000 on IEEE-69. The 0.85 value was not adjusted after seeing these results; the honest reading is that 0.85 is not a universal constant but a value whose useful range is bounded by what was tested: ρ ∈ [0.00, 0.93], bands 0.005 and 0.010, baseline margin ≈ 0.956 p.u., two radial feeders. Within that box it never produced a false alarm and caught 85–100% of violating minutes. Outside it, nothing here licenses a claim.

Where the holdout loses recall is specific and worth naming: the run at ρ = 0.369 has 33 violating minutes and Λtotal > 0.85 catches 10 of them. That placement sits at the bottom edge of the violating regime, where violation depths are 0.0008–0.0010 p.u. — the same size as the first-order model's own reconstruction residual (1.3×10−3 in u). The index cannot resolve violations shallower than its own error, and the holdout happens to contain more such marginal placements than the fitting set did. That is the whole of the degradation, not a failure of the mechanism.

What does not replicate. On IEEE-33 the ρ-dependence lives in the numerator: Dagg p95 roughly triples across the coupled range (0.0040 → 0.0140) while Derr falls. This is a real difference, and it narrows one claim from the previous version. The mechanism sentence that survives both feeders is: Λtotal rises with ρ, so violations rise with ρ. The sentence that holds only on IEEE-33 is: it rises because Dagg grows with ρ. On IEEE-69 it rises because the slack shrinks. Two topologies are not enough to say which is typical.

04

What the criterion gets wrong

Summary: one gap closed, one closed partially

The circularity objection is answered. The 0.85 threshold, applied unchanged to 28 runs it was never fitted on, holds precision at 1.000 and false-positive rate at 0.00000 in every one of them, and recall at 0.852–0.856 on the IEEE-33 holdout and 1.000 on IEEE-69, against 0.941–0.955 on the fitting set. Recall degraded; precision did not. The single-topology objection is answered for the headline result — violations rise with ρ on both feeders, with a violation-free low-coupling regime on both — but not for the mechanism attribution. On IEEE-69 the rise in Λtotal comes from shrinking certified slack rather than growing Dagg. That is a new finding from this pass, it narrows a claim rather than confirming it, and it is reported as such.

The threshold is bounded, not universal

0.85 is validated for ρ ∈ [0.00, 0.93], planning bands 0.005 and 0.010 p.u., baseline margin ≈ 0.956 p.u., 15-minute envelope refresh with 5-minute MPC re-solve, fleets of 16 and 20 vehicles, on two radial feeders. Inside that box: no false alarms, 85–100% of violating minutes caught. Outside it there is no evidence either way. It is a band- and topology-bounded operating value, not a constant, and the derivation's own threshold remains Λtotal > 1 — which on the same holdout gives recall 0.712/0.689 (IEEE-33) and 0.391 (IEEE-69) at the same perfect precision.

Missed violations are all shallower than the model's own residual

Across every set, the violations Λtotal misses have median depth 0.0005–0.0011 p.u. and maximum 0.0015, against caught-violation depths of 0.0015–0.0055. Reconstructing uac as ulin(p̄) − Dagg − Derr has mean absolute error 1.3×10−3 in u on IEEE-33 and 8.8×10−4 on IEEE-69 — the same size as the deficit at these marginal minutes. The index resolves violations deeper than its residual and cannot resolve shallower ones. The IEEE-33 holdout contains more marginal placements than the fitting set did, which is the whole of the recall gap; the IEEE-69 runs contain fewer, which is why recall there is 1.000.

Λ does not cover naive allocation, by construction

For naive one-at-a-time allocation at bands 0.005 and 0.010, Λ ≤ 1 while the AC solution violates (797 and 463 false negatives on the static derivation set; 6 and 4 on IEEE-69). Naive allocation fails for a separate and larger reason — factor-N over-allocation — that neither Derr nor Dagg models. The criterion is valid for jointly certified envelopes and under-predicts for naive ones.

The static classification stays degenerate

Once a band ≥ 0.005 exists, correctly-allocated joint envelopes violate in 0 of 1350 synthetic, 0 of 25 IEEE-33 and 0 of 25 IEEE-69 static cases. No positives, so no ROC and no fitted threshold at a static point. The closed-loop sets carry all the classification evidence.

Threats to validity

Three scope limits, stated as bounds on what the evidence supports. (a) No measured feeder data. Every closed-loop run uses synthetic load, PV and tariff profiles applied to standard test systems; nothing here has been checked against metered distribution data, and real feeders carry load volatility, unbalance and tap-changer action that this model does not represent — Dagg in particular is driven by intra-interval drift, which is exactly the quantity real data would most likely change. (b) One fleet model. A single bus type, one route structure, one timetable generator, and one charging technology; fleet size was varied (16 and 20 vehicles) and that is the only fleet dimension tested. Depot mix, mixed vehicle classes, and different dwell distributions are untested. (c) The threshold's generality is exactly what the holdout tested. Two radial feeders, two bands, one refresh cadence, the ρ range above; 0.85 carries no warrant beyond that box, and the derivation-level threshold of 1 is what should be used where the box does not apply.

06

Beyond bus fleets

Nothing in the derivation refers to buses. The criterion needs three things: connection points that share feeder resistance (ρ > 0), a load that can move between them, and an allocation computed per site and refreshed on an interval. Any resource with those properties inherits both terms.

Delivery-fleet depots. Structurally the closest case, and the drift term is larger: depot dispatch steps as vans plug in and out, and depots cluster on shared industrial laterals, so ρ runs high. τ is hours rather than minutes, which by the argument above changes γ but not the form of the criterion.

Mobile and relocatable chargers. Here ρ is a decision variable, not a network property: siting the unit chooses its shared resistance. Λtotal ≤ 1 becomes a siting constraint rather than a band-derating rule — and because Dagg carries ρ¹, siting buys more than de-rating does.

V2G and shared building storage. Bidirectional injection makes δp signed, which Dagg already handles exactly (it is linear, so sign is meaningful, and the closed loop records negative medians at low ρ). Reverse flow can raise voltage past Vmax, so the monotonicity argument of T1 must then be applied at the upper corner as well as the lower.

The portable statement: a fleet's grid impact under per-site envelopes is characterised by (ρ, band, max simultaneous draw, intra-interval drift rate γ, interval length Δ) and not by vehicle count, duty cycle or dwell time except through those five. The previous version of this work listed three of those parameters; the drift pair is the addition, and it is the pair that does most of the work at high coupling.
